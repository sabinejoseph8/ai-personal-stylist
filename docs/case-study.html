AI Personal Stylist

The problem

Online shopping gives you a search box and a thousand results that don't go together. A good stylist listens, remembers, looks at what you own, and builds a whole outfit within your budget and rules. AI chat listens, then forgets your budget and shows you the heels you ruled out.

What I built

  • My Styling Notes: the stylist jots free-form notes while you talk; a tap confirms or removes one, a correction by voice rewrites it. Nothing is a rule until confirmed.
  • Looks around what you own: a photo or the live camera names your item; three complete looks are built around it, your own pieces never in the totals.
  • One gate: every look passes a preference check (rules in code, then a model's judgment) before it reaches the screen.
  • Refinement and keeping: keep, change, swap, undo, compare; save a look; a demo bag that cannot check out.
  • Degrade, never break; privacy and budget built in: voice carries on when video can't; no recordings, photos deleted with the session, download or delete all; a daily allowance and monthly stops before every paid call.

Key decisions

  • A notebook, not a form: notes in the stylist's own words, so the conversation feels heard; the price was a reader that must be fast and right mid-sentence.
  • The stylist only speaks and listens: video and voice are swappable outside services; our server makes every decision and holds every key.
  • Accuracy first, then speed, then cost: four models measured on the same sentences before any screen was built; the "best" one was too slow for live notes.
  • Specs before code: product, design and tech specs and a phased plan, kept current; a phone check and a code review closed every phase.
The browser holds the call; our server reads notes and photos, builds looks and gates every one.

What I learned

  • Measuring before building changed the model choice; a blind set then showed how much my own cases flattered the reader.
  • Cost became a product problem: one session's judgments hit the daily allowance in my own check, so the limit, honest limit lines and a free Undo followed.
  • The automatic accessibility scan missed what measuring found: 200% text and 44 px targets caught five layout problems and one conflict in my own spec.
Limits: most answers were mine, so the scores are in-sample; the blind set of 15 sentences scored 83% right and 85% in time, below both targets: the honest estimate for new sentences. One tester, one iPhone; no stranger yet. Catalog photos, not phone photos. Taste isn't measured, only rules.
